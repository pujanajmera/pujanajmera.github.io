Research

My current research directions are broken into four main areas:

  1. IMethods for electric fields in enzymes
  2. IIElectrostatic control of electronic structure in strongly correlated systems
  3. IIIElectrostatics in controlling quantum charge transfer processes
  4. IVCollaborations in artificial metalloenzyme design
I

Methods for electric fields in enzymes

Developed and applied PyCPET (GitHub) to evaluate dynamics of 3D electric fields and electrostatic potentials in active sites.

Papers that develop and/or apply PyCPET

  1. Polarization Is Required to Model Electrostatic Preorganization: The Case of Chorismate Mutase. ChemRxiv 2026 (preprint)
  2. Distinct Electric Fields Enable Common Catalytic Function in Structurally Diverse Enzymes. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2025
  3. PyCPET: Computing Heterogeneous 3D Protein Electric Fields and Their Dynamics. J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2025
  4. Methods for Theoretical Treatment of Local Fields in Proteins and Enzymes. Chem. Rev. 2025
  5. Directed Evolution of Protoglobin Optimizes the Enzyme Electric Field. J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2024
II

Electrostatic control of electronic structure in strongly correlated systems

Understanding the role of the protein environment and electrostatics on governing the complex electronic structure of metalloenzymes active sites.

Papers

A protein-based model of carbon monoxide dehydrogenase exhibits tunable covalency across cluster oxidation and ligand-bound states. Chem. Sci. 2026

Modelling electrostatic control of dynamic electron transfer and PCET.

Empirical approaches

  1. Molecular Basis of Mitochondrial Complex I Disruption by m.14484T>C-Induced Leber Hereditary Optic Neuropathy. bioRxiv 2026
  2. Coenzyme Q10 trapping in mitochondrial complex I underlies Leber’s hereditary optic neuropathy. Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A. 2023
IV

Collaborations in artificial metalloenzyme design

My collaborative work with experimentalists surrounds designing and understanding artificial metalloenzymes from pre-existing scaffolds such as streptavidin, heme proteins, or ferredoxin. These projects are often informed by our theoretical advancements in understanding electrostatic preorganization.

Papers

Designing an Artificial Metalloenzyme for Re-based CO2 Photoreduction. ChemRxiv 2025
